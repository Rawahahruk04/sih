Headline index

Laspeyres aggregation over base-period expenditure shares of a GEKS-Jevons elementary index. Base period = 100 (geometric mean of the base window). Chain drift is removed structurally; every point below carries its sample size and coverage.

Lead-time

Two different questions, deliberately kept apart. The price curve is how much more a late booking costs than an early one (a fare level). The inflation index is how fast each window's fares are rising over time (base = 100). Only the price curve should slope.

Each window is 100 at the base period; divergence afterwards is differential inflation, not a category error to expect monotone.

Volatility & the cost of sparse sampling

The core argument for daily collection, quantified. We take the daily index as the truth, sample it the way a low-frequency process would, and measure how far the sampled reading lands from the true monthly average.

Measurement error vs sampling intensity

Mean absolute error and 95th-percentile error of the monthly reading, and how often the month-on-month direction comes out wrong, as a function of collection days per month.

Intraday fare dispersion by advance window

Within-day variation across capture slots (excluded from the index). Late-booking fares move most intraday — which is exactly why the index fixes a single daily capture slot.
